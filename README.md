## 1. Confirm Whether Apostille Is the Correct Requirement

One of the most important checks is identifying the type of legalisation required by the destination country.

An apostille is used for eligible public documents intended for use in another country covered by the Hague Apostille Convention. It authenticates the origin of the document, including the signature or seal and the capacity in which the official signing it acted. It does not certify the accuracy of the document's contents.

If the destination country does not accept apostilles under the applicable arrangements, normal attestation or a different legalisation procedure may be required.

Before beginning, applicants should confirm the requirement with the overseas employer, university, government department or other receiving organisation. The Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) also advises applicants to check whether an apostille is necessary for their particular circumstances.

## 2. Identify the Correct Route for Each Document

Not every certificate follows the same preliminary verification route. The document's category and issuing authority can affect the authentication required before it reaches the MEA.

For example:

- **Educational documents:** Degree certificates, diplomas and academic records may require verification through the relevant education authority or another designated channel.
- **Personal documents:** Birth certificates, marriage certificates, affidavits and other personal records may require authentication through the appropriate state authority.
- **Commercial documents:** Business-related certificates and documents may require preliminary verification through the relevant Chamber of Commerce or designated authority.

This distinction matters when an applicant has several documents issued by different institutions. A degree certificate and a birth certificate, for example, should not automatically be assumed to follow an identical verification route.

Applicants seeking apostille services in Bangalore should therefore prepare a document-wise list before starting the process. This helps identify separate requirements and reduces the possibility of submitting an incomplete set.

## 3. Check the Receiving Organisation's Document Requirements

An apostille may be correctly issued, but applicants can still face problems if the receiving organisation has additional document requirements.

A university may request a specific academic certificate, while an employer may ask for a degree certificate and supporting records. A government authority may require a particular version of a personal document.

Before submitting documents, confirm:

- The exact certificate or record required.
- Whether the original document or an accepted certified copy is needed.
- Whether translations are required.
- Whether supporting documents must accompany the certificate.
- Whether the recipient has specified any additional format or submission instructions.
- Whether the destination country's rules require any further step beyond apostille.

Do not assume that apostille replaces every other requirement. It authenticates the origin of an eligible public document; the receiving authority determines whether the underlying document meets its own substantive requirements.

## 4. Plan Around Deadlines, Not Just Submission Dates

Applicants often begin document legalisation only after receiving an admission confirmation, employment offer or relocation date. This can leave limited time for preliminary verification, corrections and final submission.

A better approach is to work backwards from the date by which the overseas organisation needs the documents.

For example, an applicant joining an overseas company may need to submit an educational certificate before the employer's onboarding deadline. If the certificate requires verification from its issuing authority before apostille, the applicant must account for both stages.

Keep a separate record of:

1. The date by which the receiving organisation needs the document.
2. The time required for preliminary verification, if applicable.
3. The expected submission and collection arrangements.
4. Any time needed to correct discrepancies or obtain replacement records.

The MEA's 2025 Citizen's Charter states that apostille services are usually completed within seven working days after documents are received by the MEA. This is not a guaranteed end-to-end timeline: preliminary authentication, document-specific requirements, transport and other factors can affect the overall schedule.

## 5. Understand Whether e-Sanad Applies to Your Document

Applicants may also come across e-Sanad, the Government of India's online platform for document verification and attestation or apostille.

The platform is designed to support digital processing where the relevant document is available in an integrated digital repository. Its availability is not universal for every certificate or issuing authority. The document must meet the platform's eligibility requirements.

Therefore, applicants should check whether their particular document and issuing authority are supported before assuming that the entire process can be completed online.

For current eligibility and service information, refer to the official e-Sanad portal: https://esanad.nic.in/

## 6. A Practical Checklist Before Applying for Apostille

A little preparation can make the process more organised. Before arranging submission, review the following points:

- Confirm the destination country's apostille requirements.
- Ask the receiving organisation which documents it needs.
- Identify the issuing authority for every certificate.
- Check whether preliminary state-level or other authentication is necessary.
- Review the certificate for legibility, completeness and discrepancies.
- Confirm whether originals, certified copies or translations are required.
- Check the eligibility of the document for e-Sanad, where relevant.
- Keep copies and a record of all documents submitted.
- Allow sufficient time for verification and processing.

This checklist is particularly useful for applicants handling multiple certificates for employment, higher education, family relocation or business-related requirements.
